    How it works

    A transparent, stage-wise pipeline. Each stage earns its place for a separable, measurable reason.

    1

    Query understanding

    A deterministic parser pulls out brand, colour, material, size, price range, delivery days, stock and exclusions. Multi-need queries are decomposed by an LLM.

    2

    Hybrid retrieval

    BM25 and MiniLM dense search, fused with reciprocal rank fusion (k=60). Fusion lifts Recall@100 from 0.773 to 0.840.

    3

    Cross-encoder re-ranking

    The top 50 are re-scored by ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2. The largest single nDCG gain (+0.067).

    4

    Knowledge-graph filter

    Products link to brand, colour and material nodes and carry price, delivery and stock. Hard limits sink violators without deleting them, preserving recall.

    5

    Agent + grounded answer

    A LangGraph loop self-checks, relaxes the query and retries, then a local LLM writes a recommendation citing only retrieved products.

    Reading the interface

    • Green chips are soft attributes (brand, colour, material); purple chips are hard numeric limits; red chips are exclusions.
    • A dimmed card failed at least one constraint. Its red tags say which.
    • Compare with BM25 shows how far each product moved versus the baseline ranking.
    • The Pipeline depth buttons run the exact ablation configurations A0 – A6.

    Honest limitations

    • Price, delivery and stock are synthetic. ESCI has none.
    • SC-100 is partly circular: its relevance is defined by the constraints the filter enforces.
    • Answer quality is measured on synthetic queries; the answer LLM is small and local.
    • In demo mode the UI runs your real parser, BM25, knowledge graph and filter on a 60-product sample. Re-ranking is a lexical stand-in, and answers are templated rather than written by an LLM.
